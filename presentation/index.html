01 · WHY CLAIMS ARE HARD TO ASSESS · 1:20

The art of persuasion

ILLUSTRATIVE CLAIM

“Everyone knows the real number is much higher.”

Opinion can sound like evidence.

High emotion
Fear and outrage reward quick sharing over careful checking.

Short time frame
Fact, interpretation, prediction and opinion often sit together.

Vague language
Time period, population, definition or denominator may be unstated.

Reasoning shortcuts
A vivid anecdote can overshadow broader data in a debate.

1:20. Explain how political messaging can be designed to persuade: emotional urgency, compressed formats and imprecise wording can make careful checking harder. 02 · A SIMPLE ROUTINE · 1:15

SIFT turns “is this true?” into four useful moves

S

Stop (emotions)

Pause. Restate the claim neutrally; notice the emotional pull.

I

Investigate (the speaker)

Who is speaking? What expertise, incentives or track record matter?

F

Find (other sources)

Look for independent reporting, primary records and expert sources.

T

Trace (the statement)

Follow a quote, number or image back to its original setting.

SIFT is a workflow for evaluating information—not a verdict label.

1:15. Name each step and explain the acronym. Emphasize that SIFT structures attention: it does not mechanically guarantee truth. Each move can reveal a new question for the next.

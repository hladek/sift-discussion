01 · WHY THIS IS HARD · 1:20

Political discussion compresses several problems into one sentence

“Everyone knows the real number is much higher.”

Rhetoric can sound like evidence.

Mixed content
Fact, interpretation, prediction and opinion often sit together.

Missing frame
Time period, population, definition or denominator may be unstated.

Uneven evidence
One vivid example can outweigh a broad data set in a discussion.

High emotion
Fear and outrage reward quick sharing over careful checking.

1:20. Use the quote as an invented generic example, not a quotation from the case. Point out that a statement may combine a measurable claim with an insinuation. The first task is to separate those pieces.

02 · A SIMPLE ROUTINE · 1:15

SIFT turns “is this true?” into four useful moves

S

Stop

Pause. Restate the claim neutrally; notice the emotional pull.

I

Investigate

Who is speaking? What expertise, incentives or track record matter?

F

Find better coverage

Look for independent reporting, primary records and expert sources.

T

Trace to context

Follow a quote, number or image back to its original setting.

SIFT is a workflow for evaluating information—not a verdict label.

1:15. Name each step and explain the acronym. Emphasize that SIFT structures attention: it does not mechanically guarantee truth. Each move can reveal a new question for the next.

1:30. Read the quote. The key is to define its scope before searching. Demagog’s summary reports that Eurostat counted approximately 1.27 million people found to be illegally present in the EU in 2023. 1:00. The rating is only one part of the product. Human editors choose what deserves attention, interpret the evidence and explain limitations. Invite the audience to ask what source would change the assessment.

1:30. Describe a tool-assisted pipeline rather than an autonomous truth machine. Automation is most useful for repetitive, bounded tasks. The final rating still depends on source quality, definition choices and human editorial judgment.
